\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\noindent\textbf{Setup.}
We evaluate on the full EB-Navigation test set (300 episodes, official environment, actions, and success test) and
on the full RoboTHOR ObjectNav validation split~\cite{deitke2020robothor} (\nRoboN{} episodes, 15 apartments, 12
categories; stochastic LoCoBot actuation, 30$^\circ$ turns, 500 steps, and a Stop that succeeds within 1\,m of a
visible target-category object). The models are Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct~\cite{bai2025qwen3vl}
and Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, served with vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} at temperature 0 on one 8\,GB RTX~5060 that also
renders the simulator; \ours uses their 4-bit AWQ checkpoints. The EB prompt uses the benchmark's prompts, three
examples, parser, and plan execution. Monocular depth comes from Depth
Anything V2 Metric-Indoor-Base~\cite{yang2024depthv2} on the CPU.
\textit{Protocol.} \ours was run four times and the RGB-only configuration twice, each entering with its
per-episode mean. All comparisons are paired over the 300 episodes, with bootstrap 95\% CIs and sign-flip
permutation tests, Holm-corrected over the ten variants of \cref{sec:abl}.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with published agents.}
With a 4B model, no training, and RGB alone (\nRGBThree\%), \ours exceeds every prompted MLLM up to GPT-4o and
the training-free agents built on GPT-4o and 8--35B models~\cite{huang2025esca,wang2026mosaic,ju2026embodiskill,chen2026hodagent,ma2026brainmem,lee2026emho,liang2026robofoundry}, and
MuSix and SVA, which train extra models around a 4B policy~\cite{jang2026musix,xie2026sva}. Only the tool-library
agents~\cite{zhou2026enabling} and RoboFoundry on GPT-6 Astra score higher (\cref{fig:landscape}).

\noindent\textbf{Where the gain comes from.}
The diagnosed failure modes largely disappear (i). Per run, \ours never sees the target in \nNeverSThree{} episodes (EB
prompt: \nNeverEBThree), is blocked in \nBlockedSThree{} (\nBlockedEBThree), loses a seen target in \nLostSThree{}
(\nLostEBThree), and ends near it in \nNearSThree{} (\nNearEBThree) (\cref{fig:analysis}b, \cref{fig:traj}).

\subsection{Components}
\label{sec:abl}
\noindent\textbf{Generic modular agent.}
The same model in a generic modular agent, which acts on single-view detections with the same map, planner,
look-around, and exploration, reaches \nModThree\% (\cref{tab:main}): ${+}\nModOverEB$\,pp over the EB prompt
($p\nModOverEBP$), but \nSOverMod\,pp below \ours (CI [${+}\nSOverModLo$, ${+}\nSOverModHi$], $p\nSOverModP$).
Moving the state out of the model gives part of the gain, and the evidence design for a generative sensor the rest.
\Cref{tab:abl} changes one element at a time. Disabling multi-view fusion, so that each detection replaces the
earlier hypotheses, lowers success by \nAblFusionAbs\,pp (Holm $p\nAblFusionP$),
the only component whose removal survives the correction. Fusion does not help the agent find the target;
it keeps the target once found: \nDetPrec\% of single lifted detections lie
within 0.5\,m of it, and a hypothesis there reaches commitment in \nHoldNF{} episodes without fusion and \nHoldS{}
with it. Without fusion, each detection, including the false boxes of
\cref{sec:diagnosis}, replaces the committed hypothesis, and lost targets rise from \nLostSThree{} to \nLostNF{}
episodes and near misses from \nNearSThree{} to \nNearNF.
Removing the look-around leaves the average unchanged ($\nAblScan$\,pp) but trades one subset against the others:
Long changes by $\nAblScanLong$\,pp because the agent no longer turns to find a target behind it, while the other
subsets gain the steps that the turn no longer costs ($\nAblScanOtherMin$ to $\nAblScanOtherMax$\,pp). Negative evidence, tiled search, the crop check, and the search index each change success by less than 3\,pp,
none significantly. They cost no environment steps and matter when candidates compete, which EB-Navigation's
single rooms rarely produce.

\subsection{Sensing}
\label{sec:sensing}
\noindent\textbf{Pose.}
Dead reckoning (\cref{eq:deadreckon}) is exact in heading and tilt and errs by \nDRmean\,m in position on
average, from the simulator's grid snapping. Accumulated odometry noise of 5\% per translation and 2$^\circ$ per turn changes success
by $\nAblOdom$\,pp (\cref{tab:abl}).

\noindent\textbf{Depth.}
Monocular depth with one global scale $\bar\sigma$ is not enough: with pose from the executed actions, \ours reaches \nRGBGlobal\%, level with the EB prompt ($\nGainGlobal$\,pp,
$p\nDiscGlobalP$). The global scale places target detections at a median \nRangeGlobal{} of their true range
(\cref{fig:analysis}c) and lifts \nBlockGlobal\% of the nearby floor above the obstacle threshold, so the agent
walks to a point short of the target: \nShortGlobal{} failures exhaust a hypothesis within 1\,m of it, none with
the sensor. The per-frame floor scale (\cref{eq:floorscale}) lowers the falsely blocked floor to \nBlockFloor\% and
moves the range ratio to \nRangeFloor{} (sensor: \nRangeSensor); success rises to \nRGBThree\%, ${+}\nGainRGB$\,pp
over the EB prompt ($p\nGainRGBP$) and \nGapRGBAbs\,pp below the depth sensor (Holm $p\nAblRgbfloorP$).

\subsection{Remaining gap: appearance-only goals}
\label{sec:gap}
Visual accounts for \nVisGapShare\% of the gap to the oracle and is the only subset on which \ours does not exceed
the EB prompt. The failure is recognition: a candidate comes within 0.5\,m of the target in
\nRecallOtherLo--\nRecallOtherHi\% of the episodes of every other subset but in \nRecallVisLo--\nRecallVisHi\% of the
Visual ones, and \nVisFailNoCand{} of the \nVisFail{} Visual failures never produce one.

\subsection{RoboTHOR ObjectNav}
\label{sec:robothor}
On the full RoboTHOR validation split, \ours reaches \nRoboSR\% success and \nRoboSPL\% SPL. Among zero-shot
methods (\cref{tab:robothor}) its SR is below only ForesightNav (12B~\cite{liu2026foresightnav}) and STRIVE
(Gemini-2.0-flash~\cite{zhu2025strive}), and its SPL only below STRIVE. \Cref{fig:robothor} gives each category.
