\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\textit{Benchmarks.} We evaluate on the full EB-Navigation test set (300 episodes, official environment,
actions, and success test) and on the full RoboTHOR ObjectNav validation split~\cite{deitke2020robothor} (1800
episodes in 15 apartments with 12 target categories). RoboTHOR uses a LoCoBot with stochastic actuation, 0.25\,m
steps, 30$^\circ$ turns, at most 500 steps, and an explicit Stop that succeeds if a target-category object is
visible within 1\,m.
\textit{Models.} Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, both 4-bit AWQ, served with
vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} at temperature 0 on one 8\,GB RTX~5060 that also renders the simulator.
\textit{Baseline.} The EB prompt (\cref{sec:diagnosis}) with the same local model, which isolates the interface
from the model.
\textit{Protocol.} The configuration of \ours was frozen on the development split before any test run. The main
Qwen3 configuration was run three times on the test set; the runs differ only through nondeterminism in batched
GPU inference. Component and sensing variants are single runs. Because all runs use the same 300 episodes, we
report paired statistics: episode-level bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals and exact two-sided McNemar tests,
with a Holm correction over the nine variants of \cref{tab:abl}. These variants reuse the test set, so their
$p$-values are descriptive rather than confirmatory.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:main}
\Cref{tab:main} gives success per subset. With Qwen3-VL-4B, \ours succeeds in 79.6\% of the episodes (95\% CI
[75.1, 83.8]) against 48.0\% for the EB prompt, a paired gain of $+31.6$\,pp (CI [$+24.9$, $+38.1$]). Each run
is significant on its own: run~1 solves 114 episodes that the EB prompt fails and fails 19 that it solves
($p=1.1{\times}10^{-17}$; runs 2 and 3: 115/22 and 118/22, $p{<}10^{-15}$). The gain is positive on every subset
and largest on Long-horizon, from 11.7 to 82.2\%. The three runs agree within a
point (79.7, 79.0, 80.0\%), and runs 2 and 3 differ from run~1 on 8 and 15 episodes. With Qwen2.5-VL-3B, \ours reaches
70.0\% against 6.3\% for the EB prompt with length-bounded outputs ($+63.7$\,pp, 192 episodes gained and 1 lost).
Moving from the 4B to the 3B model costs \ours 9.6\,pp but costs the EB prompt 41.7\,pp: the interface makes the
agent far less sensitive to the strength of the model.

For context, the benchmark paper reports 57.7\% for GPT-4o, 20.3\% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and 29.3\% for
InternVL3-8B~\cite{yang2025embodiedbench}. A tool library lets an 8B model reach 80\%~\cite{zhou2026enabling},
and reinforcement fine-tuning with a privileged bird's-eye view brings Qwen2.5-VL-3B to
72.3\%~\cite{wang2025seenav}. These numbers come from different inputs, evaluation subsets, or models and are not
controlled comparisons, but they place a training-free 3--4B agent that receives no target information in the
range of much larger or fine-tuned systems.

\noindent\textbf{Where the gain comes from.}
\Cref{tab:taxonomy} breaks the episodes down by outcome, and \cref{fig:traj} shows typical cases. The two failure modes that a metric controller should
remove disappear. No episode of \ours fails without ever seeing the target (18 for the EB prompt), because the
agent completes a look-around before searching, and none is blocked by collisions (43 for the EB prompt),
because motions are planned on the occupancy map. \ours also loses a target it has seen half as often (24 versus
47) and ends near the target a third as often (9 versus 26), because the target position stays in memory when it
leaves the view. On Long-horizon episodes, \ours succeeds in 48 of 60 where the EB prompt succeeds in 7.

\subsection{Components}
\label{sec:abl}
\Cref{tab:abl} removes one component at a time. Disabling multi-view fusion, so that each detection replaces the
earlier hypotheses, lowers success from 79.7 to 69.0\% ($-10.7$\,pp, CI [$-15.7$, $-6.0$]; 44 episodes lost and
12 gained), whereas the repeated reference runs lose 5 and 7 episodes relative to run~1. Among the seven
component variants it is the only one that survives the Holm correction. The loss is spread over the four subsets in which the agent starts facing the target
and is absent on Long-horizon. This is the central mechanism of \ours: single answers of a 4B model are too
unreliable to act on, and fusing them across views recovers targets that no single view gives with confidence.

Removing the look-around leaves the average unchanged but trades one subset against the others. Long-horizon
success falls by 31.7\,pp (22 versus 3 discordant episodes), because the agent no longer turns to find a target
behind it, while the other subsets gain 3.3--15.0\,pp, consistent with the steps that the turn no longer costs.
Removing exhaust-on-arrival lowers Visual by 10.0\,pp (6 versus 0 discordant episodes) and changes no other
subset by more than 3.3\,pp, consistent with appearance-only goals producing look-alikes that must be ruled out
once visited.
Negative evidence, tiled search, the full-frame crop check, and the probability-to-cost index each change success
by $-3.0$ to $+1.3$\,pp, with intervals that include zero, and strongest-first selection does as well as the
index. We keep these components because they cost no environment steps, but we do not claim a measured benefit
for them in EB-Navigation's single-room scenes, where competing hypotheses are rare.

\subsection{Sensing}
\label{sec:sensing}
\ours uses depth and odometry, while the EB prompt receives RGB and a text history of its actions. We examine
the two extra inputs separately (\cref{tab:abl}, bottom).

\noindent\textbf{Pose.}
In EB-Navigation a rejected move leaves the agent in place and turns and tilts are exact. Dead reckoning from the
executed actions and their success flags, the same feedback the EB prompt receives, therefore reproduces heading
and tilt exactly and position up to the simulator's 0.1\,m grid snapping: on the logged test trajectories the mean
position error is 0.10\,m and the median error at the end of an episode is 0.15\,m. Injecting zero-mean odometry
noise of 5\% per translation and 2$^\circ$ per turn, accumulated over the episode, leaves success at 79.3\%
($-0.3$\,pp). Pose is not what separates the two agents.

\noindent\textbf{Depth.}
Replacing simulator depth with a monocular estimate (Depth Anything V2 Metric-Indoor-Small~\cite{yang2024depthv2},
one scale calibrated on development frames) lowers success to 45.7\% ($-34.0$\,pp). The failures move after
recognition: near misses rise from 9 to 43 and lost-after-seen episodes from 24 to 63, while never-seen episodes
go only from 0 to 1, i.e., targets are found but placed at the wrong distance. Compared with the EB prompt on the same
episodes, this agent still gains $+36.7$\,pp on Long-horizon (24 versus 2 discordant episodes,
$p=1.0{\times}10^{-5}$) and loses 10.0--15.0\,pp on the other subsets ($-2.3$\,pp overall, $p=0.6$). The benefit
of turning and remembering does not depend on metric depth quality; the benefit on targets already in view does,
so recovering it without a depth sensor requires better RGB-only geometry.

\subsection{Remaining gap: appearance-only goals}
\label{sec:gap}
Visual is the weakest subset in every exact-depth run of \ours (50.0--56.7\%) and accounts for about two thirds
of the gap to the oracle. The perception logs locate the failure in recognition. A detection or hypothesis comes within 0.5\,m of the
true target in 93--98\% of the episodes of every other subset but in only 65--67\% of Visual episodes, and over the
three runs 47 of the 84 Visual failures never produce such a candidate (\cref{app:stats}). Fusion helps where
recognition works at all, raising the chance of a close candidate over an episode well above the 69--79\% of a
single named-target view, but when the 4B model cannot match a description such as ``rectangular yellowish
object with a soft and smooth surface'' to the right object, there is nothing to fuse.

\subsection{RoboTHOR ObjectNav}
\label{sec:robothor}
\Cref{tab:robothor} reports the full validation split. \ours reaches 59.6\% SR (95\% CI [57.3, 61.9]) and 35.9\% SPL
[34.1, 37.6]; the 17 episodes with simulator errors count as failures. It is above CoW~\cite{gadre2022cows},
SG-Nav~\cite{yin2024online}, and CogNav~\cite{cao2024cognav}, which use an OWL-ViT detector, LLaMA-7B, and GPT-4V,
and below STRIVE~\cite{zhu2025strive}, which pairs Gemini-2.0-flash with a dedicated open-vocabulary detector;
its SPL is close to STRIVE's. These agents also use RGB-D and pose, so the sensing is matched here, but the rows
are published numbers rather than reproductions. Per category, success ranges from 88.0\% for basketballs to
25.3\% for vases (\cref{app:robothor}).
