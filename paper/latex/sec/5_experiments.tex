\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\noindent\textbf{Setup.}
We evaluate on the full EB-Navigation test set (300 episodes, official environment, actions, and success test) and
on the full RoboTHOR ObjectNav validation split~\cite{deitke2020robothor} (\nRoboN{} episodes, 15 apartments, 12
categories; stochastic LoCoBot actuation, 30$^\circ$ turns, 500 steps, and a Stop that succeeds within 1\,m of a
visible target-category object). The models are Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct~\cite{bai2025qwen3vl}
and Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, served with vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} at temperature 0 on one 8\,GB RTX~5060 that also
renders the simulator; \ours uses their 4-bit AWQ checkpoints. The EB prompt uses the benchmark's prompts, three
examples, parser, and plan execution. Monocular depth comes from Depth
Anything V2 Metric-Indoor-Base~\cite{yang2024depthv2} on the CPU.
\textit{Protocol.} The main configuration of \ours was run four times on the test set and the RGB-only configuration twice; a repeated configuration
enters every comparison with its per-episode mean. All runs share the same 300 episodes, so all comparisons are paired. We report episode-level bootstrap 95\%
confidence intervals and two-sided sign-flip permutation tests on the per-episode differences ($10^6$ samples),
Holm-corrected over the ten variants of \cref{tab:abl}.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with published agents.}
With a 4B model and no training, \ours exceeds every prompted MLLM up to GPT-4o, the scene-graph, module-graph,
skill, memory, harness, and self-evolving agents on GPT-4o and 8--35B
models~\cite{huang2025esca,wang2026mosaic,ju2026embodiskill,chen2026hodagent,ma2026brainmem,lee2026emho,liang2026robofoundry}, and
MuSix and SVA, which train extra models around a 4B policy~\cite{jang2026musix,xie2026sva}. Only the tool-library
agents~\cite{zhou2026enabling} and RoboFoundry on GPT-6 Astra score higher (\cref{fig:landscape}).

\noindent\textbf{Where the gain comes from.}
Per run, \ours never sees the target in \nNeverSThree{} episodes (EB prompt: \nNeverEBThree), is blocked in
\nBlockedSThree{} (\nBlockedEBThree), loses a seen target in \nLostSThree{} (\nLostEBThree), and ends near it in
\nNearSThree{} (\nNearEBThree) (\cref{fig:analysis}b, \cref{fig:traj}): the look-around, the occupancy map, and the
memory of the target remove these failure modes.

\subsection{Components}
\label{sec:abl}
\Cref{tab:abl} changes one element at a time. Disabling multi-view fusion, so that each detection replaces the
earlier hypotheses, lowers success by \nAblFusionAbs\,pp (CI $[\nAblFusionLo, \nAblFusionHi]$, Holm $p\nAblFusionP$),
the only component whose removal survives the correction; the loss falls on the four subsets in which the agent
starts facing the target.
Removing the look-around leaves the average unchanged ($\nAblScan$\,pp) but trades one subset against the others:
Long changes by $\nAblScanLong$\,pp because the agent no longer turns to find a target behind it, while the other
subsets gain the steps that the turn no longer costs ($\nAblScanOtherMin$ to $\nAblScanOtherMax$\,pp). Removing exhaustion
on arrival changes Visual by $\nAblExhaustVisual$\,pp, where appearance-only goals produce look-alikes that must be
ruled out once visited.
Negative evidence, tiled search, the crop check, and the search index each change success by less than 3\,pp,
none significantly; they cost no environment steps and target competing candidates, which EB-Navigation's single
rooms rarely contain.

\subsection{Sensing}
\label{sec:sensing}
\noindent\textbf{Pose.}
A rejected move leaves the agent in place, and turns and tilts are exact, so dead reckoning (\cref{eq:deadreckon})
reproduces heading and tilt exactly and position up to the simulator's 0.1\,m grid snapping: its mean error along the test
trajectories is \nDRmean\,m. Accumulated odometry noise of 5\% per translation and 2$^\circ$ per turn changes success
by $\nAblOdom$\,pp (\cref{tab:abl}).

\noindent\textbf{Depth.}
Monocular depth with one global scale is not enough. With pose from the executed actions and depth from the network
under the scale $\bar\sigma$, \ours reaches \nRGBGlobal\%, level with the EB prompt ($\nGainGlobal$\,pp,
$p\nDiscGlobalP$). The global scale places target detections at a median \nRangeGlobal{} of their true range
(\cref{fig:analysis}c) and lifts \nBlockGlobal\% of the nearby floor above the obstacle threshold, so the agent
walks to a point short of the target: \nShortGlobal{} failures exhaust a hypothesis within 1\,m of it, none with
the sensor. The per-frame floor scale (\cref{eq:floorscale}) lowers the falsely blocked floor to \nBlockFloor\% and
moves the range ratio to \nRangeFloor{} (sensor: \nRangeSensor); success rises to \nRGBThree\%, ${+}\nGainRGB$\,pp
over the EB prompt ($p\nGainRGBP$) and \nGapRGBAbs\,pp below the depth sensor (Holm $p\nAblRgbfloorP$).

\subsection{Remaining gap: appearance-only goals}
\label{sec:gap}
Visual is the weakest subset in every run of \ours (\nVisSRLo--\nVisSRHi\%), accounts for \nVisGapShare\% of the
gap to the oracle, and is the only subset on which \ours does not exceed the EB prompt. The failure is recognition: a candidate comes within 0.5\,m of the target in
\nRecallOtherLo--\nRecallOtherHi\% of the episodes of every other subset but in \nRecallVisLo--\nRecallVisHi\% of the
Visual ones, and \nVisFailNoCand{} of the \nVisFail{} Visual failures never produce one.

\subsection{RoboTHOR ObjectNav}
\label{sec:robothor}
On the full RoboTHOR validation split, \ours reaches \nRoboSR\% success and \nRoboSPL\% SPL. Among zero-shot
methods (\cref{tab:robothor}) its SR is above all but ForesightNav, which runs a 12B
model~\cite{liu2026foresightnav}, and STRIVE, which pairs Gemini-2.0-flash with a dedicated
detector~\cite{zhu2025strive}; its SPL is second only to STRIVE and above that of the ProcTHOR policy trained
for the task~\cite{deitke2022procthor}. Per category, success ranges from \nRoboWorst\% (vases) to
\nRoboBest\% (basketballs; \cref{fig:robothor}).
