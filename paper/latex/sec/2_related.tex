\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{MLLM agents for embodied tasks.}
EmbodiedBench~\cite{yang2025embodiedbench} evaluates multimodal LLMs on high- and low-level embodied tasks through
a standardized action interface; related benchmarks probe embodied decision making and navigation
reasoning~\cite{li2024embodied,qiao2025navbench}. Agents are improved by embodied pre-training, online
reinforcement learning, or policy-level reasoning~\cite{chen2025transforming,wang2025vagen,zhang2025embodiedx,liu2025robogpt},
or at test time with reflection, experience, memory, or
tools~\cite{shinn2023reflexion,zhao2023expel,chen2024automanual,wang2024agent,lei2025robomemory,ma2026brainmem,lee2026emho,zhou2026enabling,singhi2026think}.
On EB-Navigation, models of 8B and more also call mapping tools~\cite{zhou2026enabling} or read a bird's-eye
view~\cite{wang2025seenav}. All of these agents keep the model as the planner and the reader of its own memory. In \ours the VLM is only a
sensor: its answers feed an explicit metric estimator and planner, which is what lets a 4B model drive the agent.

\noindent\textbf{Zero-shot object navigation and semantic memory.}
Modular zero-shot ObjectNav combines open-vocabulary perception, a map, and a planner, from CoW's frontier
exploration~\cite{gadre2022cows} to value maps~\cite{yokoyama2023vlfm}, LLM-reasoned scene and goal
graphs~\cite{yin2024online,huang2025msgnav,yin2025unigoal}, world models~\cite{nie2025wmnav}, cognitive state
machines~\cite{cao2024cognav}, and VLM-queried structures~\cite{zhu2025strive}; instruction-following agents ground
language in maps and scene graphs~\cite{long2024instructnav,rana2023sayplan,werby2024hierarchical}. 3D scene graphs, semantic maps, language
fields, and embodied memories attach semantics to geometry~\cite{gu2023conceptgraphs,jiang2025dualmap,qin2023langsplat,katragadda2025online,lee2026embodiedsplat,yang2024scene,anwar2024remembr,ginting2025enter}. Closest to our memory,
ApexNav fuses target-centric detections over time~\cite{zhang2025apexnav}, and AECNav treats expected but absent
detections as negative evidence~\cite{liu2026aecnav}. \ours shares the modular structure of these systems but differs in
three ways: a small generative VLM is its only perception model, so it reads open instructions instead of a fixed
category list; that model is never asked where to explore, which follows geometric rules; and its memory is a short
list of metric target hypotheses built to absorb the false boxes of a generative model.

\noindent\textbf{Verification and search.}
Spatial prompting, verification, test-time computation, and calibrated uncertainty improve VLM decisions in
embodied tasks~\cite{yang2023mark,nasiriany2024pivot,goetting2024navigation,kwok2025robomonkey,dao2026direct,choi2026scale,ren2023robots,liang2024introspective,ren2024explore}.
\ours spends extra queries, a magnified crop or a tiled view, only where the evidence is weak, and chooses among
candidate targets with the probability-to-cost index of optimal sequential search~\cite{kadane1977optimal}.

\noindent\textbf{Geometry from images.}
Learned reconstruction and depth priors recover geometry from RGB~\cite{wang2025vggt,murai2024mast,maggio2025vggt,wen2025foundationstereo,yang2024depthv2,lin2025depth},
and sensors can change embodied conclusions~\cite{corlito2026sensor,wang2025rethinking,cai2025navdp}. \ours applies the
ground-plane scale of monocular visual odometry~\cite{song2014robust} to every frame of a metric depth network.
Concurrently, Li \etal~\cite{li2026structure} run Qwen3-VL-4B zero-shot and accumulate its answers as per-direction
scores in a bird's-eye field for vision-language navigation; \ours lifts boxes to metric target hypotheses for
object navigation.
